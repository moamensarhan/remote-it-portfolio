<h1>Goal:</h1> Acquire Zendesk hands-on exposure through the available free trial. A billing dispute will be simulated to encourage the exploration and learning of various features.

<h2>What is Zendesk?</h2>

Zendesk is a customer-support platform that organizes requests into tickets. Agents can review the issue, communicate with the customer, record internal information, and track the ticket through to resolution.

<h2>Ticket features</h2>

A ticket contains information about the customer and their request, along with fields such as status, priority, assignee, and tags. These help agents understand the issue, organize tickets, and see who is responsible for handling them.

<b>Internal notes and public replies:</b> A public reply is intended for the customer. An internal note is for the support team and is not visible to the customer. These options make it important to check the selected comment type before submitting a response.

<b>Customer notes:</b> Notes on a customer’s profile can hold useful context for agents. They are separate from a ticket reply and are not visible to the customer.

<b>Tags:</b> Tags are labels attached to tickets. They can help categorize and find tickets, such as by issue type, topic, or urgency.

<b>Ticket status:</b> Status shows where the request is in the support process. For example, a ticket can be open while an agent is working on it, pending while waiting for the customer, or solved when the issue has been addressed.

<b>Priority and assignee:</b> Priority indicates how urgent a ticket is, while the assignee shows who is responsible for it. 

For this simulation, I created a billing dispute ticket and explored how ticket details, customer notes, tags, internal notes, and status can be used to document and manage the request.
